Guide to using the AI Impact Prediction Portal.

1. Project Overview

The AI Impact on Jobs Prediction Portal is an advanced analytics tool designed to quantify the susceptibility of various job roles to automation and augmentation by Artificial Intelligence.

Using a Random Forest Regression model trained on thousands of job profiles, this system analyzes key structural features of a job—such as the nature of tasks and the availability of AI tools—to predict a probability score of AI impact over the next 5 years.

2. How to Use the Dashboard

  1. Navigate to Dashboard: Go to the main 'Dashboard' tab.
  2. Enter Parameters: Input the required values in the form on the left.

    1. Impact Per Model (AI Efficacy)

    Measures the performance gap between AI and human workers. A high score means AI doesn't just assist but outperforms humans in speed or accuracy (e.g., algorithmic trading vs. manual trading).

    2. AI Models (Market Density)

    Represents the volume of competing AI solutions. High density implies a mature, commoditized market (e.g., Translation services), making it cheaper and easier for companies to replace human effort.

    3. Workload Ratio (Automation Exposure)

    Derived from 'Time-Motion Studies'. It calculates the percentage of a workday spent on digitizable tasks. If 80% of a job is data processing, it has a high exposure ratio.

    4. Tasks (Process Standardization)

    Measures the 'Routine vs. Cognitive' balance. High scores indicate repetitive, rule-based workflows (e.g., payroll processing) which are prime targets for Robotic Process Automation (RPA).

  3. Run Prediction: Click the blue "Run Prediction Model" button.
  4. Analyze Results: View the gauge on the right.
    • 0-30%: Low Risk (Safe/Human-centric)
    • 30-70%: Moderate Risk (Augmentation likely)
    • 70-100%: High Risk (Automation likely)

Metric Definitions

R2 Score (-)

Indicates how well the model explains the variance in the data. Higher is better (closer to 1.0).

RMSE (-)

Root Mean Square Error. The average deviation of predictions from actual values. Lower is better.

Pearson (-)

Measures the linear correlation between predicted and actual values. 1.0 is a perfect positive correlation.
